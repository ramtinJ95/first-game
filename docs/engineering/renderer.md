### Renderer

- `createRenderer({ canvas, map, onPick })` in `src/render/renderer.ts` is the boundary. `src/app/main.ts` calls `draw({ previous, current, progress, selection, planned, previousSight, sight })` once per animation frame, and the renderer calls `onPick` with what a click points at. It reads the state and never writes it.
- `provinceAt(map, point)` in `src/render/pick.ts` finds the province under a map point, or nothing at sea. `pickAt` checks drawn figures and ships first, then army banners, and then calls it. Both are pure, so tests and any later rule use the same function.
- `src/render/camera.ts` holds the pan and zoom maths with no PixiJS import. `drawnArmies` in `src/render/markers.ts` places each banner between the previous and the current day by the clock's progress.
- The map is drawn as named layers, in this order from the bottom: `paper`, `sea` (fill, water lines, and the moving swell), `terrain` (the land's paper, the tint of the bare ground, desert ground, the salt pans, the green strips of desert rivers and the Nile delta clipped to land, wood ground, then the owners' washes, then fields, the regional texture, and three tiers of mountains and folds, hills, trees, dunes, stipple, and the ground's marks, then the borders between provinces), `riversAndCoast` (lakes with their own water lines, cached in a texture with the terrain, then rivers and the coast), `fog` (the shade and hatching over what the player cannot see), `grain` (the paper texture, printed over the drawing), `settlements` (`towns` with the town and village symbols, then `cities`, then `life` with smoke and boats, then `armies`, the figures), `markers`, `selection`, `labels` (city, peak, town, and village names, over everything so nothing covers a name). `LAYERS` in `renderer.ts` is that list. Add to a layer and keep the order.
- The terrain and the places live in `src/render/terrain/`. `marks.ts` places every drawing as plain data in km, a `Mark`, from the map's terrain areas, peaks, rivers, and settlements. `Land` holds the map's areas as rasters (`area-index.ts`): 4 km cells answer "inside?" fast, and a chamfer distance on 8 km cells answers "how deep?" and "how far from the edge?". `draw.ts` turns marks into PixiJS drawings, `terrain-layer.ts` builds them chunk by chunk, `places-layer.ts` places towns, villages, and names, and `life.ts` draws the smoke and boats.
- A drawing is seeded from its position. `scatter` in `scatter.ts` gives each cell of a fine lattice one candidate at a hashed spot with a hashed priority, and keeps it when no candidate of higher priority stands within their spacing. That rule reads only nearby cells, so the same map always draws the same way and chunks drawn apart meet with no seam. Each mark's own details come from `stream(seed)` in `random.ts`. Nothing in the renderer calls `Math.random`.
- The terrain is drawn into a texture 256 px larger than the view on each side and shown as one sprite, like the sea, so an idle frame costs one sprite. A cached texture of a new size is a new texture (`fitTexture` in `src/render/cached-texture.ts`), because Pixi's `RenderTexture.resize` leaves a texture that has been drawn into at its old size, and the sea, the terrain, and the fog then drew scaled and offset after a window resize. It draws again when the zoom changes, the texture no longer covers the view (`coversView`), or a chunk or tier fades. A resize that leaves the old texture covering the view keeps it until then, so the frame after a resize costs no more than another: redrawing the terrain and the sea at the new size in that one frame held the screen on the old frame for seconds on a software GPU. The canvas says the size of the last frame the GPU has finished in `data-drawn-size`, and counts those frames in `data-drawn-frames`. It says the zoom of the last frame drawn in `data-zoom`, so `e2e/wheel.spec.ts` can show that every wheel notch reaches the camera, however many arrive between two drawn frames. `resizeTo(page, size)` in `e2e/support.ts` resizes and waits for that, and the capture's clip takes no screenshot while a scenario resizes, because a screenshot in hand put the old size back. The wide tier is built when the renderer starts. Each other tier is cut into square chunks (`TIERS` in `marks.ts`). A chunk is placed and drawn the first time it comes into view, in slices of 12 ms on a timer between frames, so building runs at the speed of the main thread however slow the frames are. It fades in over 260 ms and is kept. The canvas carries `data-terrain="busy"` while chunks are missing or fading and `"ready"` after, which the capture waits for. Chunks out of view are hidden. Once the camera rests for 250 ms, the chunks around the view and those of the next finer tier are built ahead.
- `DETAIL` in `src/render/terrain/detail.ts` is the zoom rule, in px per km: the widest zoom of a 1280 px view is about 0.17 and the closest is 4. Desert and wood ground and lakes show at every zoom. Wide relief (ranges as ranges) shows to 0.66 and is gone by 0.92. Small tree marks in the woods and dunes and stipple in the deserts fade in from 0.26 to 0.4 and hand over to the middle tier with the wide relief. Middle relief with trees, dunes, stipple, the named peaks, and the dry land's folds, ground, burial mounds, and river trees fades in from 0.66 to 0.92 and out from 1.75 to 2.4. Close relief with hatched shading, and the open ground's tufts, scrub, rocks, and streams, fades in from 1.75 to 2.4. The ground's tint and the salt pans show at every zoom, and the hamlets and their qanats come with the fields. Towns fade in from 0.75 to 1.05 and their names from 1.5 to 2. Villages fade in from 1.7 to 2.3 and their names from 2.7 to 3.3. Peak names fade in from 1.4 to 1.9. Fields, orchards, and tracks, and the smoke and boats, fade in from 2.2 to 3. A symbol or name that gains its place fades in over 220 ms, and one that loses it hides at once, so names never overlap.
- Mountains stand along ridges that run parallel to a range's outline, larger and denser deeper in the range and near a high peak, and leave a valley along each river. Forest trees stand in woods, clumps of overlapping round patches that are denser deeper in the forest. Woods whose ecoregion name is Mediterranean get cypresses, conifer woods get pointed trees, and the rest get round crowns. Sand seas get more dunes than stony deserts. Fields ring every place out to `FIELD_RING`, and trees keep off the inner `FARMED_SHARE` of that ring. Open land outside the wood lobes, the fields, and the watered valleys shows its ground with the close relief (`ground` in `marks.ts`, drawn in one pass by colour under the relief, so it adds no chunk where the land had none): clumps of tufts and scrub, rocks near hills and mountains and on dry land, and here and there a stream.
- `Land.cover` tells green, dry, and arid land apart from data the map already has, so the map file holds no climate. The map's forest areas are the temperate forest biomes, so land outside them is dry: steppe and Mediterranean scrub. Dry land within `ARID.desertKm` of a desert, or more than `ARID.forestKm` from every forest and `ARID.coastKm` from the sea, is arid, as the Persian plateau, Mesopotamia, and the Sahara's edge are. The cover is worked out per 4 km cell the first time a cell is asked. `groundTint` in `draw.ts` warms the bare ground under the washes from paper to straw on dry land and to buff on arid land, a raster of `TINT_CELL` km drawn larger with smoothing and clipped to the land.
- A range outside the forests is drawn from the middle zoom in as folds, as the Zagros and the Atlas are, except within reach of a high named peak (`FOLD_PEAK_LIFT`), which keeps its peaks: long ovals seen from above along the way the range runs (`Land.strike`, the doubled-angle mean of the depth's slope over a lattice round the spot, so folds run one way across a range and do not curl round each basin in it), the flank away from the light shaded and hatched, with the rings of their strata. Dry land shows its ground from the middle zoom too, burial mounds stand in groups on its plains (`TUMULUS`), and trees line its rivers (`GALLERY_KM`); tufts there are straw, not green, and arid land has more rocks and no streams (`GROUND_MIX`). A desert whose name says it is salt (`SALT_FLAT`, the Dasht-e Kavir) lays pans of white crust (`Land.pans`), cracked into polygons from the middle zoom, with no dunes or stipple on them.
- The dry land has unnamed hamlets where people lived by water, which the renderer places itself, since every settlement in the map has a name it bore in antiquity: on dry land by a river or near the edge of a range, in the valleys between a dry range's folds too, outside the woods and the fields of every place (`HAMLET`). `Land.hamletsNear` finds them on tiles of `HAMLET.tile` km, each placed the first time it is asked, so every chunk finds the same ones. A hamlet keeps a clearing and a small ring of fields like a village, with tracks to the two nearest places or hamlets; on arid land its houses are mud brick with flat roofs and its fields an oasis of orchards and green crops. A hamlet on arid land below a range with no river at hand has a qanat: a line of shaft mounds from the mother well at the nearest foot of the range down to it (`qanatTo`).
- A city always wins a place over a town, and a town over a village (`claimSymbols` in `places.ts`). Names go through `placeLabels` with `LABEL_PRIORITY`: a great power's capital, any city, a peak, a town, a village. A name tries eight spots round its symbol and keeps clear of the symbols, the banners of armies that stand still, and the battle markers. A great power's capital is always named: with no free spot it takes the first one that clears the names already placed. A spot whose name crosses open water (`FAR_SHORE_PX`) onto another shore is not near its place and is never taken, so a long name at a wide zoom is not written over another coast, and a name with no other spot is not drawn at that zoom. A capital's name takes a spot written on land before one over the sea.
- Smoke and boats run on `performance.now()`, not on the game clock, and draw only while the life band shows and only where the player sees, so a boat in the fog never passes for a hidden convoy.
- City symbols are drawn in code in `src/render/symbols.ts`, one per `city.rank`: a great power's capital, its other cities, and a minor city. They grow up to 2.5 times at the closest zoom, and the town and village symbols grow by the same factor (`symbolGrowth` in `src/render/camera.ts`), so a city stays larger than a town and a town than a village.
- The selection lives in `src/app/main.ts` and reaches panels as `view.current.selection`. It is not game state.
- Dropping a save file on the page loads it through `loadGame` and `clock.load`.

#### Natural shores and inhabited ground

- `lake-shore.ts` details every outer and island shore once, retaining all geographic control points. Fill, mask, ink and water lines share that outline; island holes cut the fill too. Sample displacement is bounded by clearance over the whole chord, so narrow inlets and nearby islands constrain the detail. Tests cover all lake rings for crossings/containment, a 0.6 km maximum shore deviation, and no straight run longer than 3 km within a 20 m chord tolerance.
- Hills have seeded width, height, skew and readable inclination around their own relief's strike; their depth index needs a nonzero 36 km reach and samples on a 12 km lattice. Dry folds keep the full range strike, with broader seeded angle, aspect, size and spacing variation. Both retain the existing chunk-overhang budget.
- `rural.ts`/`rural-draw.ts` enrich the close fields tier using existing land, cover and river data: river-attached irrigation branches and irregular reed/palm pools in coastal cultivated fans; meadow ribbons and small pastoral camps/flocks on watered open dry plains, clear of woods, relief and farm rings. These are atlas ornaments, never rules entities or navigable water. Farm tracts vary coherently with local river direction instead of a constant delta angle. [Seven-region audit, lake metrics and verification](../evidence/map-natural.md).
- Pinned-before captures use the historical revision's own save helper, not a compatibility exception in the current game.
