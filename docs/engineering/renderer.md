### Renderer

- Minors are a neutral stone grey (`MINOR_COLOUR`, `0x6e6a64`) with no hue of their own, so at the widest zoom a minor's flag never reads as Macedon's blue; the grey-blue it replaced was CIE76 63 from Macedon in full colour but of the same hue at flag size.
- At atlas scale through 0.4 px/km, `washStrength` in `palette.ts` uses a 0.60 power wash and no minor wash: the predominantly minor-held opening map keeps its ground colours. By 0.9 px/km the original light 0.34/0.14 power/minor strengths return. Owner-coloured boundaries separate unwashed minors and carry clear wash fades; the player's boundaries have a cream halo and heavier ink. The seven-ground CIE76 floor is 10 between powers and between each power and ground in full-strength interiors, not opaque relief ink. Minor/ground contrast is deliberately zero. [Contrast method and complete matrices](../evidence/map-colours.md).
- `batch-limits.ts` caps WebGL batches at eight textures before shader construction and after context restoration. SwiftShader reports 32, whose branch-heavy shaders take seconds to link; a ninth texture starts another batch, never omits art. WebGPU is unchanged. `startup:*` user timings and `node scripts/profile-startup.ts --base <preview-url> --draw full` distinguish CPU terrain/wash generation from synchronized shader-link time. [Measurements and download tradeoff](../evidence/map-startup.md).
- Historical before/after captures use `withBaseline` to build a pinned main archive without fetching media history. Its callback receives that revision's own entry, camera, draw-wait and `loadSave` helpers, so it uses the matching renderer protocol and writes the correct historical save envelope. This is capture-only; the current game's strict save-change-set checks never accept legacy formats.
- `createRenderer({ canvas, map, onPick })` in `src/render/renderer.ts` is the boundary. `src/app/main.ts` calls `draw({ previous, current, progress, selection, planned, previousSight, sight })` once per animation frame, and the renderer calls `onPick` with what a click points at. It reads the state and never writes it.
- `provinceAt(map, point)` in `src/render/pick.ts` finds the province under a map point, or nothing at sea. `pickAt` checks drawn figures and ships first, then army banners, and then calls it. Both are pure, so tests and any later rule use the same function.
- `src/render/camera.ts` holds the pan and zoom maths with no PixiJS import. `drawnArmies` in `src/render/markers.ts` places each banner between the previous and the current day by the clock's progress.
- The map draws a frame only for a cause, and `REDRAW_CAUSES` in `src/render/redraw.ts` names them all: the camera, a capture's still pose, a pause or a resume, a new state, sight, the selection, a waiting order's paths, terrain chunks and texture drawings, the fog, fades of towns and names, banners, refugee columns, the figures, merchants, and the swell. `draw` collects the causes of each frame and draws none without one. The canvas counts the frames sent to the GPU in `data-submitted-frames` and names the causes of the last in `data-submitted-because`. While the game is paused, any field of the `Frame` the page hands over that changes is a cause by itself, from `FRAME_CAUSES`, which the type checker makes name every field. A change to the scene that no cause named, found in Pixi's own lists of what changed since it last drew (`sceneChanged`), still draws, as the cause `unnamed`, and counts in `data-unnamed-frames`, which every test in `e2e/paused-drawing.spec.ts` requires to be 0. It caught the last step of each town and name fade, which `places.step` used to set without saying so.
- A paused game holds still. The swell, men at ease, fires, smoke and boats follow the time the game has run, not the wall clock, so they stop where they stand when the game pauses and go on from there when it runs. Measured with `node e2e/paused-bench.ts` before the change, a paused game at the middle zoom drew about 2 frames a second in SwiftShader on 2 CPUs and kept the browser's processes at about 1.3 to 1.8 CPU-seconds a second; after it, none, and about 0.07. A running game draws as before: the swell still steps every 80 ms while the clock runs.
- A capture sets `data-still` on the canvas while it shoots. The renderer then draws men at ease, marching steps, fires, smoke, and the swell at clock 0, says each finished frame drawn so in `data-drawn-still`, and goes back to the wall clock when the attribute goes. Two captures of an unchanged view then match pixel for pixel, which is how CI knows what a pull request changed (`scripts/capture/still.ts`, `e2e/still-pose.spec.ts`).
- The map is drawn as named layers, in this order from the bottom: `paper`, `sea` (fill, water lines, and the moving swell), `terrain` (the land's paper, the tint of the bare ground, desert ground, the salt pans, the green strips of desert rivers and the Nile delta clipped to land, wood ground, then the owners' washes, then fields, the regional texture, and three tiers of mountains and folds, hills, trees, dunes, stipple, and the ground's marks, then the borders between provinces), `riversAndCoast` (lakes with their own water lines, cached in a texture with the terrain, then rivers and the coast), `fog` (the shade and hatching over what the player cannot see), `grain` (the paper texture, printed over the drawing), `settlements` (`towns` with the town and village symbols, then `cities`, then `life` with smoke and boats, then `armies`, the figures), `markers`, `selection`, `labels` (city, peak, town, and village names, over everything so nothing covers a name). `LAYERS` in `renderer.ts` is that list. Add to a layer and keep the order.
- The terrain and the places live in `src/render/terrain/`. `marks.ts` places every drawing as plain data in km, a `Mark`, from the map's terrain areas, peaks, rivers, and settlements. `Land` holds the map's areas as rasters (`area-index.ts`): 4 km cells answer "inside?" fast, and a chamfer distance on 8 km cells answers "how deep?" and "how far from the edge?". `draw.ts` turns marks into PixiJS drawings, `terrain-layer.ts` builds them chunk by chunk, `places-layer.ts` places towns, villages, and names, and `life.ts` draws the smoke and boats.
- A drawing is seeded from its position. `scatter` in `scatter.ts` gives each cell of a fine lattice one candidate at a hashed spot with a hashed priority, and keeps it when no candidate of higher priority stands within their spacing. That rule reads only nearby cells, so the same map always draws the same way and chunks drawn apart meet with no seam. Each mark's own details come from `stream(seed)` in `random.ts`. Nothing in the renderer calls `Math.random`.
- The terrain is drawn into a texture 256 px larger than the view on each side and shown as one sprite, like the sea, so an idle frame costs one sprite. A cached texture of a new size is a new texture (`fitTexture` in `src/render/cached-texture.ts`), because Pixi's `RenderTexture.resize` leaves a texture that has been drawn into at its old size, and the sea, the terrain, and the fog then drew scaled and offset after a window resize. It draws again when the zoom changes, the texture no longer covers the view (`coversView`), a tier fades, or a chunk in view changes from what the texture holds. Each chunk keeps the alpha it was last drawn into the texture with (`drawnAlpha`), and the chunks shown are those of the whole texture, margin and all, so a pan inside the margin finds them drawn. A chunk that is built or changes out of view waits for the next drawing and has no fade; one fainter than `FAINT` counts as not drawn; and while chunks the texture shows still wait to be built, those already built wait up to `BATCH_MS` (1.5 s) to be drawn with them. At 1600 by 900 on a software GPU each drawing of the texture took about 0.8 s, and the terrain had drawn it again whenever a chunk crossed the edge of the view or stepped its fade: 68 drawings in 30 s of a pan at the middle zoom, against 15 now. A resize that leaves the old texture covering the view keeps it until then, so the frame after a resize costs no more than another: redrawing the terrain and the sea at the new size in that one frame held the screen on the old frame for seconds on a software GPU. The canvas says the size of the last frame the GPU has finished in `data-drawn-size`, and counts those frames in `data-drawn-frames`. It counts the drawings of the view-sized textures, the terrain, the sea, the coast's ink and a selected city's routes, in `data-texture-passes`, and of them the sea's in `data-sea-passes` and the routes' in `data-trade-passes`, so a test counts what takes a software GPU most of a second each instead of timing it. It says the zoom of the last frame drawn in `data-zoom`, so `e2e/wheel.spec.ts` can show that every wheel notch reaches the camera, however many arrive between two drawn frames. `resizeTo(page, size)` in `e2e/support.ts` resizes and waits for that, and the capture's clip takes no screenshot while a scenario resizes, because a screenshot in hand put the old size back. The wide tier is built when the renderer starts. Each other tier is cut into square chunks (`TIERS` in `marks.ts`). A chunk is placed and drawn the first time it comes into view, in slices of 12 ms on a timer between frames, so building runs at the speed of the main thread however slow the frames are. It fades in over 260 ms and is kept. The canvas carries `data-terrain="busy"` while chunks are missing or fading and `"ready"` after, which the capture waits for. Chunks out of view are hidden. Once the camera rests for 250 ms, the chunks around the view and those of the next finer tier are built ahead.
- `DETAIL` in `src/render/terrain/detail.ts` is the zoom rule, in px per km: the widest zoom of a 1280 px view is about 0.17 and the closest is 4. Desert and wood ground and lakes show at every zoom. Wide relief (ranges as ranges) shows to 0.66 and is gone by 0.92. Small tree marks in the woods and dunes and stipple in the deserts fade in from 0.26 to 0.4 and hand over to the middle tier with the wide relief. Middle relief with trees, dunes, stipple, the named peaks, and the dry land's folds, ground, burial mounds, and river trees fades in from 0.66 to 0.92 and out from 1.75 to 2.4. Close relief with hatched shading, and the open ground's tufts, scrub, rocks, and streams, fades in from 1.75 to 2.4. The ground's tint and the salt pans show at every zoom, and the hamlets and their qanats come with the fields. Towns fade in from 0.75 to 1.05 and their names from 1.5 to 2. Villages fade in from 1.7 to 2.3 and their names from 2.7 to 3.3. Peak names fade in from 1.4 to 1.9. Fields, orchards, and tracks, and the smoke and boats, fade in from 2.2 to 3. A symbol or name that gains its place fades in over 220 ms, and one that loses it hides at once, so names never overlap.
- Mountains stand along ridges that run parallel to a range's outline, larger and denser deeper in the range and near a high peak, and leave a valley along each river. Forest trees stand in woods, clumps of overlapping round patches that are denser deeper in the forest. Woods whose ecoregion name is Mediterranean get cypresses, conifer woods get pointed trees, and the rest get round crowns. Sand seas get more dunes than stony deserts. Fields ring every place out to `FIELD_RING`, and trees keep off the inner `FARMED_SHARE` of that ring. Open land outside the wood lobes, the fields, and the watered valleys shows its ground with the close relief (`ground` in `marks.ts`, drawn in one pass by colour under the relief, so it adds no chunk where the land had none): clumps of tufts and scrub, rocks near hills and mountains and on dry land, and here and there a stream.
- `Land.cover` tells green, dry, and arid land apart from data the map already has, so the map file holds no climate. The map's forest areas are the temperate forest biomes, so land outside them is dry: steppe and Mediterranean scrub. Dry land within `ARID.desertKm` of a desert, or more than `ARID.forestKm` from every forest and `ARID.coastKm` from the sea, is arid, as the Persian plateau, Mesopotamia, and the Sahara's edge are. The cover is worked out per 4 km cell the first time a cell is asked. `groundTint` in `draw.ts` warms the bare ground under the washes from paper to straw on dry land and to buff on arid land, a raster of `TINT_CELL` km drawn larger with smoothing and clipped to the land.
- A range outside the forests is drawn from the middle zoom in as folds, as the Zagros and the Atlas are, except within reach of a high named peak (`FOLD_PEAK_LIFT`), which keeps its peaks: long ovals seen from above along the way the range runs (`Land.strike`, the doubled-angle mean of the depth's slope over a lattice of 30 km steps round the spot, so folds run one way across a narrow range such as the Zagros and do not curl round each basin in it, while broad uplands such as those round Lake Urmia turn with their own outline rather than share one grain for hundreds of km), the flank away from the light shaded and hatched, with the rings of their strata. Dry land shows its ground from the middle zoom too, burial mounds stand in groups on its plains (`TUMULUS`), and trees line its rivers (`GALLERY_KM`); tufts there are straw, not green, and arid land has more rocks and no streams (`GROUND_MIX`). A desert whose name says it is salt (`SALT_FLAT`, the Dasht-e Kavir) lays pans of white crust (`Land.pans`), cracked into polygons from the middle zoom, with no dunes or stipple on them.
- The dry land has unnamed hamlets where people lived by water, which the renderer places itself, since every settlement in the map has a name it bore in antiquity: on dry land by a river or near the edge of a range, in the valleys between a dry range's folds too, outside the woods and the fields of every place (`HAMLET`). `Land.hamletsNear` finds them on tiles of `HAMLET.tile` km, each placed the first time it is asked, so every chunk finds the same ones. A hamlet keeps a clearing and a small ring of fields like a village, with tracks to the two nearest places or hamlets; on arid land its houses are mud brick with flat roofs and its fields an oasis of orchards and green crops. A hamlet on arid land below a range with no river at hand has a qanat: a line of shaft mounds from the mother well at the nearest foot of the range down to it (`qanatTo`).
- A city always wins a place over a town, and a town over a village (`claimSymbols` in `places.ts`). Names go through `placeLabels` with `LABEL_PRIORITY`: a great power's capital, any city, a peak, a town, a village. A name tries eight spots round its symbol and keeps clear of the symbols, the banners of armies that stand still, and the battle markers. A great power's capital is always named: with no free spot it takes the first one that clears the names already placed. A spot whose name crosses open water (`FAR_SHORE_PX`) onto another shore is not near its place and is never taken, so a long name at a wide zoom is not written over another coast, and a name with no other spot is not drawn at that zoom. A capital's name takes a spot written on land before one over the sea.
- Smoke and boats run on `performance.now()`, not on the game clock, and draw only while the life band shows and only where the player sees, so a boat in the fog never passes for a hidden convoy.
- City symbols are drawn in code in `src/render/symbols.ts`, one per `city.rank`: a great power's capital, its other cities, and a minor city. They grow up to 2.5 times at the closest zoom, and the town and village symbols grow by the same factor (`symbolGrowth` in `src/render/camera.ts`), so a city stays larger than a town and a town than a village.
- The selection lives in `src/app/main.ts` and reaches panels as `view.current.selection`. It is not game state.
- Dropping a save file on the page loads it through `loadGame` and `clock.load`.

#### Natural shores and inhabited ground

- `shore.ts` still details lake shores without changing their geographic controls. `coast.ts` supplies the earlier bounded sea ripples, now inherited at build time in protected places. `atlas-coast.ts` uses the renderer-only `drawing-coast.json` from Natural Earth 1:10m land at 0.1 km tolerance, unpacked lazily at 0.6 px/km. Land paper, all terrain and wash masks, water lines and coast ink share this outline. An 8 km coastal band limits changes, with the inherited coast kept at cities, harbours, narrow crossings and frame joins. At atlas zooms one land mask, a child of the transformed cached art, clips the whole terrain, ground included; below 0.6 px/km it clips only the ground, as before. The water lines and the coast and river ink are stroked only for the view and two `SEA_MARGIN`s round it (`strokeCoast`), since only their cached textures show them, and again once the view leaves that rectangle; a stroke of the whole 1:10m coast on every zoom step cost the GPU millions of vertices it never showed. The map, province outlines, mesh, harbours, physical province-hit geometry and saved state do not move. [Source, continuous displacement bounds, exceptions and evidence](../evidence/coast-shapes.md); [earlier ripple metrics](../evidence/coast-names.md).
- `coastal-ground.ts` fits drawn ground contacts to the displayed surface without writing to the state. Land figures also require physical land; ships require drawn water. Exact `AreaIndex.spanInside` queries reject even subpixel inlets while cancelling shared province seams. A uniform-raster fast certificate must reject all seam cells; otherwise a tiny water cut can hide between two inside endpoints. Fitting preserves sprites and updates hits and army boxes; an over-wide symbol shrinks only after a local full-size placement fails. Convoy followers can regroup at their army's interpolated anchor, ships at their convoy (`sceneAnchor`), which is at sea or in harbour while an embarking army's banner still walks to the port, and refugees at their nearest column, since their people trail back to the city they left in a straight line that can cross the sea. Lakes count as land for this fitting, as they do for the rules that march armies over them; the drawing of lakes is unchanged, so an army in Lake Van stands where it stood before. A banner stands on its army's surface (`fitBanner`). On the days an army embarks, sails and lands, its banner is afloat exactly where its interpolated point is off the physical land, because on the first and last of them it is drawn across the shore. Siege works and wakes stop at their surface. Refugee cart markers below figure zoom and cold-sheet fallback banners use the same protection; scenery has no physical province or land-order target. A town or village whose ground line is not all on drawn land stands at the nearest spot inland where it is (`fitPlace`), within `PLACE_REACH_KM` (12 km) along the rules' land, so an island town never crosses to the mainland; if none fits it is drawn at three quarters or half its size, and only if still none fits is it not drawn at that zoom. Its name follows it, and its map coordinates do not move. Ambient boats whose contacts do not fit are not shown.
- Cities fit their complete ground stroke with `AreaIndex.boxInside`, stay full-size and keep their rules coordinates. Visible glyph hits and labels follow the inset symbol; original city command anchors are lower-priority fallbacks, never ahead of visible cities or armies/battles. Scene-only city-position views keep wall breaches, rooftop effects and ambient smoke attached, but evaluate visibility at original positions and never leave rendering. Placement chooses its shoreline from the current camera zoom, not old cached art identity. Held frames preserve the completed snapshot without relayout.
- `cacheDrawing` in `cached-texture.ts` caches static river/coast ink into a view-sized texture with a 256 px margin, preserving its own stroke transform and the existing layer order. An idle frame submits a sprite instead of the detailed world's vertices. Covered pans/resizes reuse it; zooms or uncovered views redraw it. WebGL restoration invalidates this ink cache and schedules a paced redraw even when paused. Destruction releases the art, sprite and texture.
- Small settlement names use `SETTLEMENT_NAME`: ink rather than the old village ink/paper mix, weight 600, and the existing opaque 3 px cream halo. Villages stay 10 px italic and towns 11 px italic, below 12 px normal-weight italic city labels and 14 px bold upright capitals; priorities and fades do not change. Bolder glyph widths can affect which neighbouring label fits. The solid-core contrast floor is 7:1 against the immediate halo on every underlying ground (11.68:1 measured), not against dark relief ink without a backing. [Every ground and close-owner-wash contrast](../evidence/village-contrast.md).
- Hills have seeded width, height, skew and readable inclination around their own relief's strike; their depth index needs a nonzero 36 km reach and samples on a 12 km lattice. Dry folds keep the full range strike, with broader seeded angle, aspect, size and spacing variation. Both retain the existing chunk-overhang budget.
- `rural.ts`/`rural-draw.ts` enrich the close fields tier using existing land, cover and river data: river-attached irrigation branches and irregular reed/palm pools in coastal cultivated fans; meadow ribbons and small pastoral camps/flocks on watered open dry plains, clear of woods, relief and farm rings. These are atlas ornaments, never rules entities or navigable water. Farm tracts vary coherently with local river direction instead of a constant delta angle. [Seven-region audit, lake metrics and verification](../evidence/map-natural.md).
- Pinned-before captures use the historical revision's own save helper, not a compatibility exception in the current game.

#### Camera completion and picking

- `data-latest-view` increments on camera input, focus, restored views, resize, and context restoration. `data-drawn-view` and `data-drawn-state` advance only when the GPU has finished a frame with all visible scene sprites painted; submitting a frame is not completion. `data-zoom`, `data-drawn-size`, and `data-drawn-frames` describe completed frames even while sheets are still painting.
- Picking and right-click commands use the camera, viewport, city boxes, banners, battles, and figure boxes from one completed frame, never the pending camera. A held frame leaves that snapshot unchanged. Input also polls the existing fence non-blockingly, so a frame that finished between animation callbacks is immediately pickable. During the first asynchronous figure-sheet paint, banners remain visible and pickable until the replacement scene is ready.
