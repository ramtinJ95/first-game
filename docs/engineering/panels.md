### Panels

- `src/ui/Hud.svelte` is the one root the page mounts. It lays out one grid over the map: the top bar, the overview under it, and the alerts at the bottom left, with the side column of the army, city, and battle panels at the right edge under the top bar, which spans the width. From 1024 px the top bar keeps one row: each store and the clock stand as two short lines, a store's note is a word or two, and its full reason is the store's tooltip (`storeNote`). The `?frames` readout sits at the bottom right. Below 720 px the side column becomes a sheet at the bottom, and while it holds a panel the corner alerts give way to it, but the Chronicle button above them stays. While the Chronicle is open they give way to it too: it holds the same news, and at 390 × 844 the corner left under it was a strip shorter than one line of news. Shut, the corner on a phone is `7.4rem` tall, the Chronicle button and a line of news of four lines; a longer line scrolls. A panel open in the middle, such as Advances or Wars, takes it from them as well, even news that stopped the game, because at 390 × 844 a stopping line grew the corner and left the Advances panel 168 px of its 1,391. Below 1024 px the top bar's buttons wrap onto a row of their own rather than shorten their words, so at 390 a war, a threat, an advance at work and a store short leave every button whole; the Game menu then opens below the whole bar across its width, its lines wrapping (`e2e/layout.spec.ts`, the `phone-top-bar` capture). The side column scrolls inside itself. The grid lets clicks through to the map, and only the panels take them. The clock, the stores, and the speed buttons are never covered. The refusal notice and the right-click menu float above it. Panels read `view.current` and act only through `Actions` in `src/ui/actions.ts`, which issue orders to the clock, select, and move the camera.
- A panel prop that holds the game state is named `game`, because Svelte reads `$state` next to a local named `state` as a store.
- A right click reaches `onCommand` in `src/app/main.ts`. The renderer names the city whose symbol or label the click is on or within 10 px of, as `Command.city`, and a symbol wins over a label. `planCommand` in `src/app/command.ts` then decides what the click does with the selected army: march to the city or the point, or, when the army stands at one of its ports and the click is on another port, a menu of the ways by sea and by land that the rules allow, with their days. It plays each order on a copy of the state through `executeOrder`, so what it offers and refuses is the rules' answer, and it refuses at once in words what the rules would refuse. An order onto land that no city stands on, such as the islet of Carthago's province off Cape Bon or Corsica, is refused with the islet's words, or for land of 1,000 km² or more the island's, because an army lands only at a city's harbour (`citylessLandKm2` in `src/sim/world/land-reach.ts`). A left click there still opens the province's city, as a click anywhere in a province does. The ways it weighs are the march and the quickest way by sea (a march of at most `MAX_MARCH_DAYS` to a port of the army's owner or to the nearest shore, the days that gather ships on a shore, the crossing, and the march on from the landing, which the embark order carries as `then`), and it drops a way that takes more than `FAR_LONGER` (1.5) times the days of the quickest. A siege order counts current besiegers and the player's other armies marching or sailing to the city after queued movement orders. It names each arrival and the first army's time alone, with losses explicitly unpredicted. An issued march uses its actual route; each menu option carries its own siege outlook. A click whose march is dropped opens the menu with the way by sea, and a click with no way left is refused with the reason.
- The army panel names the city of its owner a retreat ends at, as "Retreating to Roma", and says "Falling back into the field" for a retreat that ends at none, so it never names the enemy city the army fled from (`activityText` in `src/ui/describe.ts`). Its Join section offers nearby armies through `joinChoices` and the rules' `joinRefusal`; the Guide explains that idle armies stay separate until joined.
- An army the panels call "at" a city stands within `AT_CITY_KM` in `src/app/names.ts`, which is `SIEGE_DISTANCE`, so an army at an enemy city at war besieges it. The army panel says why an idle army at a foreign city does not besiege it, and an idle army within `ENGAGEMENT_DISTANCE` of an enemy city but too far to besiege it says so and offers Besiege, the march to the city (`whyNotBesieging` in `src/ui/describe.ts`). An idle army at peace by a foreign city offers Declare war on its owner through `WarStatus`, the city panel's own act and confirmation (`declareOn`).
- The clock keeps one waiting order per army and slot: a movement slot (`moveArmy`, `halt`, `embark`, `retreat`) and a stance slot. A new order replaces the one in its slot. Orders that name no army wait side by side. The top bar counts the armies with an order waiting.
- The battle panel shows the stance buttons and the retreat for each of the player's armies in the battle, through `StanceControls.svelte`, which the army panel shares. An army in battle shows them only in the battle panel, which always stands under it, so they show once. Each stance's line comes from `STANCE_EFFECTS`. Beside each stance it says what the stance beats and what beats it (`stanceMatchShort`, from `STANCE_BEATS`), each other stance's button says what a change costs (`stanceChoiceText`, from `REFORM_DAYS`), and an army that re-forms says how many days are left (`reformingText`). In a battle one line says whose stance beats whose (`standingText`, by the battle's own `edgeBetween`), and the button of the stance that beats the enemy's is marked. The battle panel's rows give each army's stance and whether it re-forms, and the guide lists the stances from the same functions. The army and city panels list the other armies in sight at the same place.
- The Game menu's Start screen asks first, with the game paused (`askToLeave`, the `leave` modal): save and leave, which writes the `manual` slot, leave without saving, or stay, which runs a game that was running. A player who has lost goes straight to the start screen.
- `src/ui/WarsPanel.svelte` is the one place for peace: each of the player's wars and how it stands (`warRoomOf` in `src/ui/war-room.ts`), the enemy's offer with Accept and Refuse, one-click **Send offer** suggestions and terms to write with **Send these terms**, each with the enemy's answer from `peaceAnswer` and its reasons, the truces with their days left, and under At peace how near each great power at peace with the player stands to the strength it needs to declare war on it (`standingThreats` in `src/app/threat-news.ts`), red from the news's `THREAT_WARN`. It is the `wars` panel of `panel` in `main.ts`, and both the top bar's Wars button and the overview's wars open it. The Wars button counts the player's wars, marks incoming, unexpired offers, and, while a great power at peace with the player stands at `THREAT_WARN` or nearer, adds "· 1 threat" in the warning colour (`powersNearWar` in `src/app/threat-news.ts`). It reckons nothing of its own: `readinessOf` keeps each power's readiness per state, the day's news reckons it inside the clock's frame as it has since the warning came, and the top bar and the wars panel read that same state afterwards. At 1024 px, long store notes get their own line so orders and all buttons remain on one row. An AI's offer to the player is a `peaceOffer` turning point. `onDay` pauses the clock on the offer's day and opens the wars panel, which shows the war of `unansweredOffer` in `src/app/modals.ts` (an incoming offer with no answer or counter-offer queued). The offer is not a modal, so the × closes the panel, Overview replaces it, and an answer waits in the panel for the next day, all with the game still paused. When `cheaperCounter` finds terms the enemy would accept today that cost the player less than its demand, the offer says so and sends them with one click. The city panel's war confirmation lists what a war sets in motion (`declarationFacts`), and the panel at peace says how a war starts and ends (`PEACE_TEXT`, which the guide repeats). The city panel's `WarStatus` says how long a truce holds in place of the button that declares war.
- The top bar's Overview, Wars, and Advances, and the Chronicle button above the news, open one panel at a time over the map's left side, which is page state (`panel` in `main.ts`). The Advances button names the advance studied and its days left, or "None studied" in the warning colour ([Advances](advances.md)). The Overview is a list of the player's cities, armies, and wars (`overviewOf` in `src/ui/describe.ts`). A click on a row calls `actions.show`, which selects and centres the map. A city's rank line comes from `rankText`, which reads the starting owner from the map. Once the player is defeated, `overviewOf` gives only the day the realm fell, and the panel lists nothing.
- The renderer also takes `onCommand` for a right click and offers `focus(point, covered)` for an alert's click, which centres the point in the part of the map that the screen insets `covered` leave clear. `main.ts` measures the top bar, the overview, and the side column for it each time, so what was chosen never lands under a panel. A left click that finds a province or nothing also takes the city whose symbol or name it is on or within `CITY_HIT_MARGIN` of (`cityAt`), so a click on a name selects that city. A click on a city's symbol itself picks the city before figures or banners, including a convoy over it; Armies here in the city panel reaches the other selection. A camp's tents and stacks of arms answer to their army like its men, so a click on them selects the army, not the province under them. In the `markers` layer it draws the player's march and sea paths, the paths of orders still waiting, and a battle marker that `pickAt` selects after banners and before provinces. An army at sea is drawn at its convoy.
- `alertsOfDay(before, after, events)` in `src/app/alerts.ts` works out the alerts of a day, and the clock's `onDay` feeds it every tick with the tick's events and the player's orders it played. News of something that happened, such as a battle, a retreat, a lost army, desertion, or a declaration of war, comes from the events, so a battle that begins and ends inside one tick is still told. Only standing changes, such as a city's owner, a new siege, a mustered formation, an arrival, or an empty store, are read by comparing the two days. Alerts are page state, not game state. To add a kind, add a JSON file listing its names in `src/app/alert-kinds/` and one test to `alerts.test.ts`.
- An offer, refusal, and peace are alerts from `peaceOffered`, `peaceRefused`, and `peaceMade`. An incoming gold demand shows the player’s current treasury and affordability in both panel and news (`peaceFundsText`); Accept is disabled if the terms can no longer be met. A higher demand names the previous price and measured changes, including a treasury cap, in both places. Absolute peace dates use the shared year/day form; durations remain day counts. A refusal says once who refused, the current reason, and what changed if its acceptance forecast went stale. Expiration or a side's destruction emits `peaceOfferEnded`. A peace between two other great powers is told too.
- The page keeps every alert of the game, newest first, up to `CHRONICLE_KEPT` in `src/app/page.ts`, as the chronicle. The corner list shows the latest five and the Chronicle panel all of them.
- An alert carries `pause` when its news may stop the game: an important battle of the player begins (`battle`), a siege of the player is too weak to starve the city (`weakSiege`, the test of `weakSiegeNews`), an enemy first nears the player's land in a war (`sighted`, once per war by `pause.once`), an own city is besieged, in danger, or lost (`ownCity`), or a great power at peace warns that it could soon declare war (`warThreat`, which comes once a peace from each power). Agreed after the eighth play-through, in which a player at speed met both of Year 1's warnings only in the news and Egypt's 99-day warning not at all. While the corner holds news that stopped the game, it grows to `min(62vh, 30rem)`, so the stopped line is read to its last word at 1280 × 720 and 1024 × 720; the `paused-news-fits` capture asserts that for the longest text of each kind that stops the game. News told the same day can stand above the stopped line, so the corner scrolls once to the newest stopped line when it comes, and leaves the scroll to the player after (`AlertList.svelte`). `stopsOfDay` in `src/app/pauses.ts` marks the alerts that stop the game `paused`, by the Game menu's settings, which local storage keeps across games, all on by default. `onDay` then pauses the clock, which runs no further day of the frame, so the game stops on the day of the news. The camera stays where it is, and the alert says "Paused". `?pauses=off` keeps the news from stopping the game.
- `importantBattle` measures a battle by the enemy's men at contact, not the player's: it pauses for `BATTLE_PAUSE_MEN` (500) enemies, or for fewer that have `BATTLE_PAUSE_ODDS` (half) of the player's men in the fight and `BATTLE_PAUSE_SHARE` (a tenth) of all the player's men under arms. A sally of a few hundred against a siege of 1,900 stays quiet, a sally that could break the siege stops, and a battle of the last small army still stops. `node scripts/sim.ts sallies [-- --men 1900]` prints Rome's battles at the siege of Mediolanum under the old and new rules. The event carries each army's men before blows, so even a one-day battle is judged before losses. A small battle's beginning and retreat/destruction details stay in the Chronicle; its result is one neutral corner alert. The page's saved `told` keys remember `quiet-battle:<id>` through multi-day battles and reloads, then remove the key when the battle ends. `cityDanger` warns at seven days of food or seven days until wall collapse at the current besiegers' strength, only for a blockading siege, and again only after leaving and re-entering danger. Bare walls can surrender on any day, so waiting until zero is too late. The danger alert names relief, a sally with troops already at the city, and peace.
- While a great power stands near war with the player (`powersNearWar`) and the game is paused, as a war warning leaves it, each troop card the player can order says what the troop does to each power near war (`recruitThreats` in `src/ui/recruit-threats.ts`), in the guards line's words (`share`, `verdictText` and `musterVerdict` in `src/app/threat-news.ts`): its share now, the day after the order once the gold is paid, and the day after the men muster, on their day, with whether that leaves it at 80% or more, so it may still declare from its named day, or takes it below. Men who muster on or after the first day it may declare are said to come too late, men who muster beyond `WAR_FRONT_KM` of the city it weighs are said not to count, and a day before then on which the rules end its notice, as men already in training muster, the treasury falls, or guards stop counting, is named. The order is paid through the rules' handler, and each day to the muster is played by the rules' own `SYSTEMS` with no orders, so the shares are those a game in which no power gives an order would show, and the notice is the one the `notice` system keeps; no march, battle, or order a power may give is foreseen. The same days are also played once with no new order, for all the cards of a city, as far as the longest muster asked: each card gives the share its troop leaves beside the share without it ("against 78% without them"), and a notice those days end without the troop is said to end "with or without them", or later without them when the troop brings it sooner, so no card credits its troop with a fall it does not cause. Agreed after the thirteenth play-through, whose every card named the same day Carthage fell below 80%. Each day plays on fresh collections, as a tick's own copy does, because the AI caches by the state's collections. `recruit-threats.test.ts` orders each troop and plays the rules to the muster, and each share matches to the percent and each verdict to the notice the rules keep, with a store run short, armies regaining morale, a garrison refilling, a band of guards that stops counting, and a falling treasury. The cards stand in one column while it shows. Six troops cost about 300 ms in Node on a new game, so the panel reckons only while the game is paused, as a war warning leaves it, one troop a task, and while the clock runs a line above the cards says to pause to see it; a day with no power near war costs nothing. Until its task has run, each card the player can order says it is reckoning what the troop does to the threat, and when the orders waiting for the next day already take every power near war below 80%, each says so once reckoned, so no card stands silent at a warning, and a card the rules would refuse says why, an enemy at the walls included, from the checks of the rules' recruit order in its order (`refusal` in `CityPanel.svelte`). Agreed after the thirteenth play-through, whose Heavy cavalry, the last card and the longest to play, gave neither. Agreed after the eleventh play-through, whose tester saw Carthage fall from 81% to 74% two days after ordering Spearmen and to 65% the day after they mustered, and took the men for both.
- Recruitment shows one realm forecast above the troop cards (`recruitForecast` without a hypothetical troop), including training and waiting orders. Each card keeps only its upfront cost, men, training days and daily upkeep. A risky selection switches that shared forecast to include the selected troop. When a warned card names a day more than `MOVED_DAYS` (30) later than it named before, it says what moved it (`movedText`): the troops raised since, who are fed first, and the cities lost since, from the realm each reading counted on (`NamedWait`). The page keeps the first reading per city and troop until that raise is given or no longer warns; it is not saved. In the eighth play-through, 500 Skirmishers moved a Spearmen day from Year 3, day 109 to Year 10, day 21. That is right: the Spearmen leave a gap of 3.75 food a day, the Skirmishers add 3.00, and the people's growth closes only 0.34 a day each year, so the stores carry the gap for years more; playing it out passes on Year 10, day 28 at a check every 10 days. That was at the 2 % a year the people grew then. At 4 % the growth closes 0.68 a day each year, so the same card names Year 1, day 252 for the Spearmen and Year 5, day 35 after the Skirmishers, 3.4 years later instead of 6.8 (`npm run sim -- slide` measures the realm over ten years). A card that raises at once closes any confirmation left open (`confirming` in `CityPanel.svelte`), which would otherwise reckon its troop on top of the raise just queued and warn of a second regiment. A food or gold shortage at any distance is red and requires an explicit Raise anyway, but the runway grows the player's people by `YEARLY_GROWTH` as the population system does while food lasts, so a small deficit that growth closes before the stores run out stays plain and says so beside its fixed-yield days. A warned raise also names its wait, `holdsAfter`: the fewest days, up to ten years, by which putting the same raise off leaves no shortfall, because the stores fill and the people grow in the meantime. The delayed raise pays its gold on the day it is given, and each day is tried while other recruits are still training, since a later muster can lose granary room the stores needed; after that, halving finds the day. The warning then says to raise in that many days, on the day it names, or now only if the men are needed sooner, and what the wait counts on: no other troops raised and no city lost, and for a gold shortfall no gold spent and an income that holds, with trade gold that comes as each caravan arrives and can move the day by some weeks. In a world where nothing else happens the raise passes on that very day (`npm run sim -- raise-wait`); what moves the day is the player's own spending and raising, a war, or lost trade, which the forecast does not predict, and keeps "Raise fewer troops or improve income first" for a raise that no wait carries. When food holds a warned raise back and one of the player's cities could start Canals today, the city raising first and the others by id after (`canalSite`), the card adds one line (`canal`): the city, the Canals' price and food a day, and the day the raise is fed with them, from the same reckoning with the Canals' food added from the day after their days of work (`Muster.gain`). It shows only when the Canals bring the day sooner or make a raise possible that no wait carries, because the eighth play-through's Spearmen waited seven years for a gap one Canals closes; `recruit-forecast.test.ts` replays the named day with Canals ordered on the first day. When a card reads "Needs N more manpower" and one of the player's cities could start Barracks today, the city raising first and the others by id after (`barracksSite`), the card adds one line (`barracks`) in the shape of the Canals line: the city, the Barracks' price, the men more to call up and the manpower a day they give (`buildingGain`), their days of work, and the day the raise can be given with them. It plays the manpower as the economy fills it, each day's gain stopping at the call-up limit, both growing with the people and with the Barracks from the day after their work (`manpowerByDay`), and the treasury, which pays the Barracks now and must hold the raise's gold on its day. It names the first day both are in the stores, and none if gold or food runs out before it, since men who starve or go unpaid desert and the people shrink, which the days played do not foresee; the treasury may fall after. It shows only when the Barracks bring that day sooner than waiting, or let a raise that waiting never does within ten years; whether the men are fed once raised is the warning's to say when the card is clicked. `recruit-forecast.test.ts` replays the named day with Barracks ordered on the first: the raise is taken that day and refused the day before. Decided after the thirteenth play-through, whose Rome sat at its call-up limit from Year 7 with a full treasury and was never told Barracks would help. A forecast that recedes while a careful player keeps cancelling is the stores filling, not growth closing the gap: raising into it still starves men, so the warning stays and tells the day instead of reading as a refusal. A fixed horizon cannot tell the two apart: a second regiment that empties the granary in a year and a half and a gap of one or two food a day that shows 2,800 days at today's yields both look distant, but only the first outgrows the people. `npm run sim -- recruit-warning` plays a cautious Rome that heeds every warning under each rule; its table is in [warnings evidence](warnings-evidence.md). Runway pays waiting orders and this order now, starts each recruit's upkeep on its muster day (including barracks training), and caps gains by the economy's limits, which grow with the people too. It counts each building at work in the player's cities, a waiting `build` order's too, from the day after its last day of work, with the food and gold the economy gives with it and without it, today and in the grown realm (`buildingsAtWork`), and counts the days on from the end of the city's unrest, as construction works none of them. So a player who builds the Canals a card names finds, on the day it named, a card that says the men are fed, as the thirteenth play-through's did not. Balances and limits are linear in the people, so the forecast reads one grown copy of the realm and matches the economy and population systems played day by day. Future construction, conquest, battles, and losses are deliberately not predicted. Battle news names a city-state's side as "the men of" its city; "at their city" applies only when that side still owns the named battlefield city. `desertionNews` sums actual desertion events into one shortage episode, with start, 10% of the initial army lost, and end/total notices, rather than daily spam. The optional saved `Page.desertion` preserves that reckoning across reloads. The top bar's standing line names hunger and/or unpaid wages while men can still desert. Both sides of a pending Join acknowledge it in the army panel; march forecasts count the joined formations on the trial state.
- An alert is dated by the day played, the day the rules stamp on what happened in it (`fellOn`, `defeatedOn`, a war's `since`), and the list writes it with `dateText`, as the screens do.
- `sightingsOfDay` tells of a convoy or a marching army of a power at war with the player that comes into sight on its way to the player's land (within `THREAT_KM` of a city of the player, or on its provinces), with its men and the city it heads for when the player sees that place. `weakSiegeNews` tells on the day a siege of the player's is too weak to cut the food, and again once its besiegers have lost `BLED_SHARE` of their men in it, to sallies or a storm, which the page cannot tell apart; the page keeps what it told in `weakSieges`. `alertsOfDay` tells when an evacuation leaves and when its people settle, and the overview lists the columns on the road.
- `groupAlerts` folds the alerts of one kind with the same text at one place on one day into one line with a count. The page runs it after `alertsInSight`. A besieged city that musters its recruits early (`earlyMusters`) is one `musteredEarly` alert, to its owner and to the player besieging it, in place of the muster line of each formation it mustered early. A recruit that was due that day anyway keeps its own line.
- An alert covers the player's own armies and wars, and the battles, lost armies, and desertion of others that the player sees. The end of a battle gives its days and each side's losses. Desertion is told on the first day the owner is short. `alertsInSight` drops the rest. An alert selects a battle or an army only while it still exists.
- `src/ui/CityTrade.svelte` is the city panel's trade section ([Trade](trade.md#what-the-player-is-told)). Its partner list (`trade-partners`) replaces the old partner and transport dropdowns: one scroll box of the cities that agree now, each way a button with its payback, price, gold still needed and risk mark, then the cities that would not with the reason. The chosen way's quote follows it, with the payback set against the gravest risk, and the Open button names the city and the way. A running route gives its gravest risk that is not this city's own siege, and Close route asks first (`trade-close-confirm`). An invitation (`trade-invitation`) gives its terms and last day, and Accept is disabled with the reason when the rules would refuse it or accepting would end the invitation. The confirmation in `WarStatus.svelte` lists the routes a war closes (`war-trade`) under its facts. The list fits a 390 px phone inside the bottom sheet.
- The city panel gives an own city's gold and harvest from `cityBalance` in `src/ui/describe.ts`, which runs `dailyBalances` on a realm of that city alone and cuts its gold and manpower by `adminShareOf` for the owner's realm, so it restates no rate and the cities of a realm add up to its balance. Its walls and food are out of `fullDefences(city)`. For a minor's city it says in plain words what a war with it meets (`minorText`): rich and walled or poor and open, a still-independent league partner, and its army at the city while the player sees it. An unleagued city with standard walls says so; it is called a standing-army city only when the player sees its army, since an unpaired league can also be plain. `stormText` gives the storm confirmation the chance to take the city and the men it is expected to cost, from `stormOdds` in `src/sim/ai/situation.ts`. `sackText`, `storeNote` (a store at its `limitsOf` limit, or the days a falling store lasts), and `weakSiegeText` (besiegers fewer than `BLOCKADE_ODDS` times the garrison, with the men still needed) are the other lines, each with its unit test. `weakSiegeText` says nothing while a besieger is out of sight.
- A fate chosen in the prompt is an order, so it waits for the next day. No prompt, panel, or answer runs the clock: a game the page stopped for news, an offer, a fate, or a win stays paused until the player resumes it. Only the player's own words run it again once the modals close (`resumeWhenClear` in `main.ts`): Stay in the Leave dialog, when the player was running the game as they opened the Game menu, and Play on after the win. The page then says when the fate happens, and the city panel shows the fate ordered in place of its choices.
- `src/ui/CityGuards.svelte` is the own city panel's guards section, after the city's own rows (defences, unrest, siege, armies here) and before its buildings: the guards held and the day each band leaves in `dateText`'s form, how many great powers still count against a war and until when (`weighedLine`), the rule in a sentence (`GUARD_RULE`), and Hire for a year with its price, the garrison before and after, and, for each great power whose front is the city, its readiness now and with the band until the band stops counting, from `cityGuards` in `src/ui/guards-text.ts`, which plays the hire through the rules on the day the waiting orders leave, with a refusal in words. The gold store's full-treasury note points to it. When a band leaves one of the player's cities, `guardNewsOfDay` in `src/app/guard-news.ts` gives one neutral `guardsLeft` alert for the city, with the men who left and how long fresh guards count against a war, at the city and without a pause.
- `src/ui/CityBuildings.svelte` is the own city panel's buildings section, after the guards: what stands, each first level at its level and the great work, the work under way with its cancel, and the options in three groups, first levels, second levels, and great works (a city holds one), each with its effect for this city today, price, days, and payback. [Buildings](buildings.md) gives the rules it reads.
- `reasonText` in `src/app/names.ts` turns a rule's refusal into words a player reads. The notice shows it for four seconds.
- The view times each refresh as the user-timing measure `panels`. The `panels` capture scenario reads it.
- `e2e/support.ts` builds saves for browser tests and capture scenarios, such as a battle or a fallen city. Tests drop them on the page and then play through the panels.
