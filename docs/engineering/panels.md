### Panels

- `src/ui/Hud.svelte` is the one root the page mounts. It lays out one grid over the map: the top bar, the overview under it, and the alerts at the bottom left, with the side column of the army, city, and battle panels at the right edge under the top bar, which spans the width. From 1024 px the top bar keeps one row: each store and the clock stand as two short lines, a store's note is a word or two, and its full reason is the store's tooltip (`storeNote`). The `?frames` readout sits at the bottom right. Below 720 px the side column becomes a sheet at the bottom, and while it holds a panel the corner alerts give way to it, but the Chronicle button above them stays. The side column scrolls inside itself. The grid lets clicks through to the map, and only the panels take them. The clock, the stores, and the speed buttons are never covered. The refusal notice and the right-click menu float above it. Panels read `view.current` and act only through `Actions` in `src/ui/actions.ts`, which issue orders to the clock, select, and move the camera.
- A panel prop that holds the game state is named `game`, because Svelte reads `$state` next to a local named `state` as a store.
- A right click reaches `onCommand` in `src/app/main.ts`. The renderer names the city whose symbol or label the click is on or within 10 px of, as `Command.city`, and a symbol wins over a label. `planCommand` in `src/app/command.ts` then decides what the click does with the selected army: march to the city or the point, or, when the army stands at one of its ports and the click is on another port, a menu of the ways by sea and by land that the rules allow, with their days. It plays each order on a copy of the state through `executeOrder`, so what it offers and refuses is the rules' answer, and it refuses at once in words what the rules would refuse. The ways it weighs are the march and the quickest way by sea (a march of at most `MAX_MARCH_DAYS` to a port of the army's owner or to the nearest shore, the days that gather ships on a shore, the crossing, and the march on from the landing, which the embark order carries as `then`), and it drops a way that takes more than `FAR_LONGER` (1.5) times the days of the quickest. A siege order counts current besiegers and the player's other armies marching or sailing to the city after queued movement orders. It names each arrival and the first army's time alone, with losses explicitly unpredicted. An issued march uses its actual route; each menu option carries its own siege outlook. A click whose march is dropped opens the menu with the way by sea, and a click with no way left is refused with the reason.
- The army panel names the city of its owner a retreat ends at, as "Retreating to Roma", and says "Falling back into the field" for a retreat that ends at none, so it never names the enemy city the army fled from (`activityText` in `src/ui/describe.ts`). Its Join section offers nearby armies through `joinChoices` and the rules' `joinRefusal`; the Guide explains that idle armies stay separate until joined.
- An army the panels call "at" a city stands within `AT_CITY_KM` in `src/app/names.ts`, which is `SIEGE_DISTANCE`, so an army at an enemy city at war besieges it. The army panel says why an idle army at a foreign city does not besiege it, and an idle army within `ENGAGEMENT_DISTANCE` of an enemy city but too far to besiege it says so and offers Besiege, the march to the city (`whyNotBesieging` in `src/ui/describe.ts`).
- The clock keeps one waiting order per army and slot: a movement slot (`moveArmy`, `halt`, `embark`, `retreat`) and a stance slot. A new order replaces the one in its slot. Orders that name no army wait side by side. The top bar counts the armies with an order waiting.
- The battle panel shows the stance buttons and the retreat for each of the player's armies in the battle, through `StanceControls.svelte`, which the army panel shares. An army in battle shows them only in the battle panel, which always stands under it, so they show once. Each stance's line comes from `STANCE_EFFECTS`. Beside each stance it says what the stance beats and what beats it (`stanceMatchShort`, from `STANCE_BEATS`), each other stance's button says what a change costs (`stanceChoiceText`, from `REFORM_DAYS`), and an army that re-forms says how many days are left (`reformingText`). In a battle one line says whose stance beats whose (`standingText`, by the battle's own `edgeBetween`), and the button of the stance that beats the enemy's is marked. The battle panel's rows give each army's stance and whether it re-forms, and the guide lists the stances from the same functions. The army and city panels list the other armies in sight at the same place.
- The Game menu's Start screen asks first, with the game paused (`askToLeave`, the `leave` modal): save and leave, which writes the `manual` slot, leave without saving, or stay, which runs a game that was running. A player who has lost goes straight to the start screen.
- `src/ui/WarsPanel.svelte` is the one place for peace: each of the player's wars and how it stands (`warRoomOf` in `src/ui/war-room.ts`), the enemy's offer with Accept and Refuse, one-click **Send offer** suggestions and terms to write with **Send these terms**, each with the enemy's answer from `peaceAnswer` and its reasons, and the truces with their days left. It is the `wars` panel of `panel` in `main.ts`, and both the top bar's Wars button and the overview's wars open it. The Wars button counts the player's wars and marks incoming, unexpired offers. At 1024 px, long store notes get their own line so orders and all buttons remain on one row. An AI's offer to the player is a `peaceOffer` turning point that pauses the game and opens the panel on that war. The city panel's `WarStatus` says how long a truce holds in place of the button that declares war.
- The top bar's Overview and Wars, and the Chronicle button above the news, open one panel at a time over the map's left side, which is page state (`panel` in `main.ts`). The Overview is a list of the player's cities, armies, and wars (`overviewOf` in `src/ui/describe.ts`). A click on a row calls `actions.show`, which selects and centres the map. A city's rank line comes from `rankText`, which reads the starting owner from the map. Once the player is defeated, `overviewOf` gives only the day the realm fell, and the panel lists nothing.
- The renderer also takes `onCommand` for a right click and offers `focus(point, covered)` for an alert's click, which centres the point in the part of the map that the screen insets `covered` leave clear. `main.ts` measures the top bar, the overview, and the side column for it each time, so what was chosen never lands under a panel. A left click that finds a province or nothing also takes the city whose symbol or name it is on or within `CITY_HIT_MARGIN` of (`cityAt`), so a click on a name selects that city. A click on a city's symbol itself picks the city before figures or banners, including a convoy over it; Armies here in the city panel reaches the other selection. A camp's tents and stacks of arms answer to their army like its men, so a click on them selects the army, not the province under them. In the `markers` layer it draws the player's march and sea paths, the paths of orders still waiting, and a battle marker that `pickAt` selects after banners and before provinces. An army at sea is drawn at its convoy.
- `alertsOfDay(before, after, events)` in `src/app/alerts.ts` works out the alerts of a day, and the clock's `onDay` feeds it every tick with the tick's events. News of something that happened, such as a battle, a retreat, a lost army, desertion, or a declaration of war, comes from the events, so a battle that begins and ends inside one tick is still told. Only standing changes, such as a city's owner, a new siege, a mustered formation, an arrival, or an empty store, are read by comparing the two days. Alerts are page state, not game state. To add a kind, add a JSON file listing its names in `src/app/alert-kinds/` and one test to `alerts.test.ts`.
- An offer, refusal, and peace are alerts from `peaceOffered`, `peaceRefused`, and `peaceMade`. An incoming gold demand shows the player’s current treasury and affordability in both panel and news (`peaceFundsText`); Accept is disabled if the terms can no longer be met. A higher demand names the previous price and measured changes, including a treasury cap, in both places. Absolute peace dates use the shared year/day form; durations remain day counts. A refusal says once who refused, the current reason, and what changed if its acceptance forecast went stale. Expiration or a side's destruction emits `peaceOfferEnded`. A peace between two other great powers is told too.
- The page keeps every alert of the game, newest first, up to `CHRONICLE_KEPT` in `src/app/page.ts`, as the chronicle. The corner list shows the latest five and the Chronicle panel all of them.
- An alert carries `pause` when its news may stop the game: a battle of the player begins (`battle`), a siege of the player is too weak to starve the city (`weakSiege`, the test of `weakSiegeNews`), and an enemy first nears the player's land in a war (`sighted`, once per war by `pause.once`). `stopsOfDay` in `src/app/pauses.ts` marks the alerts that stop the game `paused`, by the Game menu's settings, which local storage keeps across games, all on by default. `onDay` then pauses the clock, which runs no further day of the frame, so the game stops on the day of the news. The camera stays where it is, and the alert says "Paused". `?pauses=off` keeps the news from stopping the game.
- An alert is dated by the day played, the day the rules stamp on what happened in it (`fellOn`, `defeatedOn`, a war's `since`), and the list writes it with `dateText`, as the screens do.
- `sightingsOfDay` tells of a convoy or a marching army of a power at war with the player that comes into sight on its way to the player's land (within `THREAT_KM` of a city of the player, or on its provinces), with its men and the city it heads for when the player sees that place. `weakSiegeNews` tells on the day a siege of the player's is too weak to cut the food, and again once its besiegers have lost `BLED_SHARE` of their men in it, to sallies or a storm, which the page cannot tell apart; the page keeps what it told in `weakSieges`. `alertsOfDay` tells when an evacuation leaves and when its people settle, and the overview lists the columns on the road.
- `groupAlerts` folds the alerts of one kind with the same text at one place on one day into one line with a count. The page runs it after `alertsInSight`. A besieged city that musters its recruits early (`earlyMusters`) is one `musteredEarly` alert, to its owner and to the player besieging it, in place of the muster line of each formation it mustered early. A recruit that was due that day anyway keeps its own line.
- An alert covers the player's own armies and wars, and the battles, lost armies, and desertion of others that the player sees. The end of a battle gives its days and each side's losses. Desertion is told on the first day the owner is short. `alertsInSight` drops the rest. An alert selects a battle or an army only while it still exists.
- The city panel gives an own city's gold and harvest from `cityBalance` in `src/ui/describe.ts`, which runs `dailyBalances` on a realm of that city alone and cuts its gold and manpower by `adminShareOf` for the owner's realm, so it restates no rate and the cities of a realm add up to its balance. Its walls and food are out of `fullDefences(city)`. For a minor's city it says in plain words what a war with it meets (`minorText`): rich and walled or poor and open, a still-independent league partner, and its army at the city while the player sees it. An unleagued city with standard walls says so; it is called a standing-army city only when the player sees its army, since an unpaired league can also be plain. `stormText` gives the storm confirmation the chance to take the city and the men it is expected to cost, from `stormOdds` in `src/sim/ai/situation.ts`. `sackText`, `storeNote` (a store at its `limitsOf` limit, or the days a falling store lasts), and `weakSiegeText` (besiegers fewer than `BLOCKADE_ODDS` times the garrison, with the men still needed) are the other lines, each with its unit test. `weakSiegeText` says nothing while a besieger is out of sight.
- A fate chosen in the prompt is an order, so it waits for the next day. Closing the prompt runs a game that the fate stopped, but a game paused by other news of the day, or by the player, stays paused. The page then says when the fate happens, and the city panel shows the fate ordered in place of its choices.
- `reasonText` in `src/app/names.ts` turns a rule's refusal into words a player reads. The notice shows it for four seconds.
- The view times each refresh as the user-timing measure `panels`. The `panels` capture scenario reads it.
- `e2e/support.ts` builds saves for browser tests and capture scenarios, such as a battle or a fallen city. Tests drop them on the page and then play through the panels.
