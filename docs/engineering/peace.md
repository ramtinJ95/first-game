### Peace

- A war remembers what it needs to be reckoned: `since`, `declaredBy`, and for each side in `sides` the other side's cities it took in the war and still holds (`taken`) and the men it lost in battle against the other (`lost`). `newWar(by, against, day)` in `src/sim/state/war.ts` makes one. `recordCapture` and `recordBattleLosses` in `src/sim/peace/war-record.ts` keep it, called from `changeCityOwner` and the battles system. A city won back is no longer the other side's gain.
- `warStanding(state, faction, enemy)` in `src/sim/peace/standing.ts` is the one place that says how a war stands for a side: the `CITY_POINTS` of the cities each side took and holds, `SIEGE_SHARE` of each city under siege, and up to `BATTLE_POINTS` for the men lost in battle, from facts the player can see.
- Terms (`PeaceTerms` in `src/sim/state/peace.ts`) are peace as things stand or a payment in which one side, the payer, gives gold, cities, or both. `termsPoints` in `src/sim/peace/terms.ts` prices them, `GOLD_PER_POINT` gold or a city's `CITY_POINTS` to a point. `termsProblem` refuses gold the payer lacks, a city it does not hold, and its last city, so a treaty never ends a faction and destroys no army.
- The orders are `offerPeace`, `acceptPeace`, and `refusePeace`. An offer waits in `state.peaceOffers`, one per war, for `OFFER_DAYS`, and replaces the other side's offer as a counter-offer, but not one made the same day. `makePeace` in `src/sim/peace/make-peace.ts` pays the terms, ends the war, and starts a truce in `state.truces` of `truceLength(paid)` days (`src/sim/peace/truce.ts`): `TRUCE_DAYS` (365) plus `TRUCE_DAYS_PER_POINT` (30) for each point the terms were worth to the receiver before they were paid, at most `MAX_PAID_TRUCE_DAYS` (730) more. The length lives only in the truce's `endsOn`, so no new saved field or save change is needed, and the panel's truce dates already read it. `declareWar` refuses during a truce, and the AI's war declarations, the coalition against a leader and a minor's league included, skip a faction it keeps a truce with (`truceBetween`).
- `homecoming` in `src/sim/systems/homecoming.ts` runs first in the day, after the orders, so no order given before the peace undoes it. On the peace's day it takes both sides' armies out of the battles they fight against each other and the sieges of each other's cities, and turns home every army and convoy of either side on the other's land or bound there (`sendHome` and `sailHome` in `src/sim/peace/home.ts`). On each day of the truce an idle army on the other's land goes on home, so one that marched to a shore takes ship. An army stands on the land of the owner of the city nearest it (`landOwnerAt`).
- The AI's rule is in `src/sim/ai/peace.ts`. `peaceWish` adds minus the standing, weariness, other wars, shortages, and minus resolve, with each reason in words. Its visible restraint floors a **durable** loser's wish at zero: city/battle points, excluding sieges, must be negative, and the side must have lost a still-held city or more than `SMALL_LOSS_SHARE` (10%) of its soldiers. The share uses recorded battle losses divided by surviving soldiers plus those losses. Mere siege pressure, a token clash, or sacrificing the player's own men cannot buy free early peace from an undamaged winner. When neither side lost a city or more than that share, the war is even: white peace waits `EVEN_WAR_DAYS` (225), even with shortages or other wars, except when this side is durably losing another great-power war by that same test of real losses. After that both take peace as things stand. Before 225 days, a side not durably losing here or on another great-power front keeps a wish at most −1: a second war may discount the price, but the enemy’s own casualties cannot make it free. The other-front exception only removes that cap; the remaining reckoning must still accept. Its factor names the other enemy. Talks still start at `MIN_WAR_DAYS` (60).
- `peaceAnswer` accepts when the wish and the terms' worth reach zero and talks are open. `proposal` still needs terms both sides take, and offers white peace in a mature even war. A winning side opens at its least plus `OPENING_MARGIN` (5 points, `src/sim/ai/thresholds.ts`) or halfway to the most the enemy would give, whichever is less, rather than at that most. A great power and a minor both run `peaceOrders` first each day; an AI answer rechecks current facts at execution, after any earlier orders. A sent offer keeps the forecast the player saw, including loss shares, so a changed refusal names changed captures, battle losses, sieges, force growth, or other pressures. The panel warns when a battle or siege can change its answer.
- Refusals have a required reason, told once in alerts and Chronicle. `termsProblem` uses recorded faction/city names rather than raw ids in its explanations. An offer cannot replace either side's same-day offer; the first stands and the duplicate is explicitly rejected. Expiration and destruction of a side emit `peaceOfferEnded` instead of silently losing an offer; its alert kind has its own registration file.
- The immutable save-change registration is `src/sim/save-changes/peace-forecasts-and-pending-answers.json`, not a number or an edit to an older registration. It covers sent forecasts, pending peace commands, and paused state. Saves from before still carry `resumeOnClose`, which loading now ignores, because no offer runs the clock again. A loaded unanswered offer reopens the wars panel, paused; a queued answer is not asked again. A refusal says what the refuser wants the right way round: a payer would give less, a side that is paid or paid nothing wants more (`wantedText`). No new persistence of ordinary army/recruit queues is promised. [Evidence and review-driven limits](peace-evidence.md) include the old and rebased campaign tables and the cheapest controlled truce cases.
- Each `WarSide.lastOffer` keeps only that side’s latest offer, after refusal or expiry and through save/load. The offer records both reckonings and `receiverGold`, the receiver’s treasury constraint. `offerPriceRise` compares the same sender’s preceding demand, reuses `forecastChanges`, and names a changed treasury or other pressures. A manually raised price with no measured explanation says so explicitly. This saved shape has its own immutable registration, `peace-offer-price-history.json`; old registrations are unchanged.
- `npm run sim:peace [-- --seeds 20 --years 10]` plays the new game under the AI and prints how wars end, how long they run, how soon a pair fights again, and the days each power spends at peace. `node scripts/peace-two-fronts.ts` plays the tester's case of Rome on two fronts in year 2. `npm run sim -- truce [-- --seeds 20 --years 10]` plays an idle Rome that accepts every offer it can pay against one that refuses every offer, and prints how long each paid peace holds before the same power declares again, the day Rome falls under each policy, and how far each priced offer to Rome asks above the least its sender would take.
