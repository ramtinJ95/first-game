### Faction AI

- `decideOrders(state, world, faction): Order[]` in `src/sim/ai/decide.ts` gives the orders one faction gives today. It takes a `DeepReadonly<GameState>`, never writes it, and draws no random numbers. Ties go to the lower id, so the same state gives the same orders. `decideAll(state, world, skip = [state.player])` gives the orders of every other faction that is not defeated, in faction id order.
- The clock calls it. `createClock` takes `decide(state)`, and each tick plays the player's queued orders and then `decide(state)` on the day being played. `src/app/main.ts` passes `(state) => decideAll(state, world)`.
- Every threshold is a named constant in `src/sim/ai/thresholds.ts`. Budget: `RESERVE_DAYS`, `MIN_BALANCE_SHARE`, `TROOP_MIX`. War: `WAR_ODDS`, `PEACE_WAR_ODDS`, `SATED_EXPONENT`, `RIVAL_ODDS`, `HOARD_ODDS_SHARE`, `RIVAL_PRESSURE_ODDS`, `WAR_DISTANCE_KM`, `WAR_REACH_KM`, `WAR_FRONT_KM`. Offence: `GARRISON_ODDS`, `ATTACK_ODDS`, `DEFENDER_KM`, `SEA_KM_FACTOR`, `STORM_MIN_CHANCE`, `STORM_MAX_LOSS`, `MARCH_MORALE`, `RALLY_WAIT_DAYS`. Defence: `RETREAT_ODDS`, `RELIEF_ODDS`, `RELIEF_KM`, `CAPITAL_GUARD_KM`. Fates: `ENSLAVE_BELOW_MANPOWER`. Minors: `MINOR_DEFICIT_DAYS`. The doc comment on each rule's function in `src/sim/ai` states the rule.
- A great power's day runs in this order: stances, retreats, fates, storms, army movements (capital guard, relief, offence), one recruit or else at most one building, at most one declaration of war. A minor gives orders only at war. Every faction sets the stance of each army in a battle to the one with the best `stanceEdge` over the stances of the enemy's men (`stances` in `src/sim/ai/stance.ts`): freely once in the battle, after that only while the enemy's stances beat its own and no enemy is re-forming, and never while it re-forms itself. It recruits its cheapest troop, keeps its armies at its city once their morale is back to `MARCH_MORALE`, and retreats from a battle it is losing. A minor starts with stores for its cheapest troop and a year of that troop's upkeep (`STARTING_STORES` in `new-game.ts`), so at war it fields a formation that meets the first besieger.
- A power weighs a neighbour by that neighbour's armies on land within `WAR_FRONT_KM` of its nearest city plus that city's garrison, with hired guards counted only for `GUARD_DETER_DAYS` (90) from their hire (`weighedGarrison` in `src/sim/guards.ts`), so a rival whose armies are away invites war. It holds back all of an enemy's armies for each war it fights, but not its garrisons, which never leave their walls. Against the leader, a great power with `LEADER_SHARE` of all cities and more than any other, it needs no more than `COALITION_ODDS`. Against a rival great power it needs no more than `RIVAL_ODDS`, and a power past `ADMIN_CITIES` needs less against a rival whose treasury is full: the odds fall by `HOARD_ODDS_SHARE` (0.75) times the share of the rival's treasury that is full times the share of its own cities past `ADMIN_CITIES`, so a power of 16 cities needs three eighths less against a full treasury. Past that number of cities a realm's gold arms no more men than a smaller rival's, and the gold is what a peace would make the rival pay. Against a minor it needs its odds times its cities over the cities the map gave it, raised to `SATED_EXPONENT`, so a realm that has grown is slower to start a new war on a minor. A power at war with no great power also presses a great-power neighbour that has kept to its homeland once its **uncommitted** armies outweigh that neighbour's `RIVAL_PRESSURE_ODDS` times, keeping back the greater of each existing front’s field strength and its city’s `BLOCKADE_ODDS` garrison requirement. This is a planning heuristic in the same strength points as normal garrison scoring: a broken or absent field army does not make an unfinished conquest free. Normal war selection still reserves enemy field armies only. So a player who stays at home is still threatened. Every march the AI orders, the guard of the capital, a relief, and the way back to the muster city included, goes through the router, which prices no march longer than `MAX_MARCH_DAYS` and sails where the land is too far. An army sets out for a city only with `GARRISON_ODDS` times its garrison, counting the armies sent there with it, and only when the men there by the time it arrives, those besieging and those sent there whose road and sea are no longer than its own, hold the blockade. Armies already marching on a target together keep their course only when the first of them to arrive holds the blockade. It leaves for the muster city a siege that cannot hold the blockade, unless armies marching there, and given no other order that day, bring it up to the blockade before the sallies cut down half its men, and those armies keep their march. With no way to the muster city, it steps out of the siege onto land `STEP_OUT_KM` from the city. The muster city is never a besieged one.
- An army that no road and no port of its own takes to its muster city, such as one beaten overseas, sails from the shore it stands on, or marches to the nearest shore first (`wayFromShore` in `src/sim/ai/armies.ts`), and at peace the AI brings home an army that stands on land with no city of its owner. Every crossing the AI orders carries the march on to its target.
- An army of fewer than `REMNANT_MEN` is a remnant: it takes no part in an attack, a relief, or a group, and goes to the muster city, where recruits join it. An idle army marches on an enemy army that stands idle in the field, away from its own cities, within `PREY_KM`, when it outweighs it and every enemy army beside it `ATTACK_ODDS` times.
- Armies become one only by the `joinArmy` order (`src/sim/orders/join-army.ts`): an idle army joins another idle army of its owner within `RALLY_DISTANCE` of it, under the better general, while neither re-forms (`joinRefusal`), and when their stances differ the army re-forms from that day. The player joins with the army panel's Join buttons (`joinChoices` in `src/ui/describe.ts`), so two of the player's armies can stand apart at one city. The AI joins its idle armies at each of its cities that were given no order that day or the same orders, into the lowest id (`withJoins` in `decide.ts`). Each join is weighed against the joins before it, so an army whose join cannot run keeps its orders, and the guard of the capital joins only an army that stays. A recruit joins the idle army at its city, so a power gathers at the muster city and marches and sails as one army.
- The guard of the capital (`guardOfCapital` in `src/sim/ai/armies.ts`) holds an army there only while the enemy armies within `CAPITAL_GUARD_KM` that are not at their own cities could take it: with the men to blockade its garrison, or with any chance to storm it by `stormOdds`. A raid the walls hold alone pins no army. The guard may hunt an enemy army standing within `CAPITAL_GUARD_KM` of the capital, and an army on that hunt stays the guard until it fights.
- The AI caches each collection's sorted order, each faction's enemies, each army's strength, and the day's balances in `WeakMap`s keyed by the record objects. Never change a state's collections after the AI has read it.
- `npm run sim:ai [-- --seeds 20 --years 5]` plays the new game with every faction under the AI, then with Rome giving no orders, and prints the tables the thresholds were tuned on. Both `sim:ai` and `sim:world` print, from `scripts/standoffs.ts`, the armies that stand idle beside an enemy for more than 30 days and the armies idle overseas for as long, with those the menu gives no way home. `scripts/sim-ai-conquest.ts` adds the conquest and economy section by year and the five targets for a world that lasts, each marked met or not.
- `npm run sim:opening [-- --power <id>]` plays the scripted opening of each great power on 5 seeds: war on the nearest minor city on day 1 with the starting army, and the battles, siege, and fall that follow.
- `node scripts/sim-fates.ts` prints what a fallen city adds under each fate, for three sizes over one, two, and five years, and when it is lost on day 90. `node scripts/sim-quiet.ts [-- --seeds 10 --years 5]` counts the days on which the player, Rome, gets no news in the corner list, as the page works the news out, and the day war is first declared on Rome, for a Rome that gives no orders and one under the AI.
- `npm run sim:world [-- --from 1 --seeds 20 --years 10]` plays the new game under the AI and prints, for each seed, the cities of each power, who is largest, who is alive, the sieges begun again after the power ordered its besiegers away, the armies lost to sallies, the longest stretch a power at war sat still with an army that could fight, and the first city each power took. `npm run sim:recruit` has Rome raise every troop it can afford for two years and counts the days it waits on gold and on manpower.
- `warReadiness(state, world, faction, against)` in `src/sim/ai/war.ts` gives the same reckoning for one pair: the power's odds over the odds it needs, the share the other's treasury takes off, and the other's city it weighs. A great power declares war on the player only once it has stood on notice, at `NOTICE_SHARE` (0.8) of the strength it needs against the player, for `NOTICE_DAYS` (30) days in a row (`src/sim/ai/thresholds.ts`). The `notice` system (`src/sim/systems/notice.ts`) keeps that day in each faction's `noticeSince` and forgets it on a day the power stands lower, is at war with the player or keeps a truce with it, and `warDeclaration` checks it on both paths, the odds and rival pressure. Wars between AI factions are not held back. `threatNewsOfDay` in `src/app/threat-news.ts` warns once a power has stood on notice `THREAT_STAND_DAYS` (7), because most strengths that reach the notice are gone within days and a stop for each taught the ninth play-through that doing nothing works, and it names the first day the power may declare if it holds that strength; the At peace line names it too. Two or more powers that warn on one day are one stopped line that names each with its share and its first day of war, and leaves what lowers each to the At peace list, because two whole warnings do not fit the corner at 1280 × 720. It warns of each notice once it has stood its week, whatever it warned of before, and again while it stays on that notice once `THREAT_REMIND_DAYS` (60) have passed since the day its last warning named; a reminder, whose first day of war has passed, says it is the threat already warned of and still standing ("Carthage still stands near war with you, as warned on Year 9, day 172: …"), that the power may declare any day now, and stops the game as the warning does. Agreed after the eleventh play-through, whose reminder read word for word like a first warning, so a player who had answered the first could not tell a failed answer from a new threat. It is one line longer than the first warning at 1280 × 720, 12 lines by field armies and 13 by a city, and `paused-news-fits` shows both read whole in the corner. Since a war needs 30 days on notice in a row, every war on the player follows a warning of its own notice 24 days or more before it, and comes no more than 60 days after the day it was last told of. Agreed after the tenth play-through, in which Macedon came back on notice 316 days after its last warning, a recall of 335 days held its warning back to the 335th day, and it named a day 11 days off; and Carthage declared 152 days after the day it named. Over 20 ten-year games of `npm run sim -- threat-runup`, 4 of 45 wars came more than 60 days after that day on a notice held since its warning, and the reminder that ends them comes 7 times, 0.35 stops a game. Trade consent also foresees a war on the day a notice runs out. A war or a truce forgets the warning, and the saved `told` keeps its day as `threat:<power>:<day>`. When a notice the player was warned of ends at peace and the power then stands off notice for `THREAT_STAND_DAYS` (7) days in a row, the day it ended counted, `threatFadedNewsOfDay` tells it once, on that seventh day, in a line that does not stop the game ("Carthage's threat has passed for now: it has fallen to 49% of the strength it needs to declare war on you, and must stand at 80% for 30 days before it may."), so a player who answered a warning learns that the danger passed. Where the share would read 0%, or there is none, the line gives the reason instead (`whyFaded`): a power holds back the whole field strength of each enemy it fights within reach (`heldBy` in `warReadiness`), so one whose wars outweigh all its armies stands at exactly 0 ("it is at war with Egypt, which ties down all its armies"), and a share above 0 that rounds to 0 reads "under 1%". A power with no army idle or marching that can fight, as when all are in battle, in sieges or at sea, weighs no war at all ("none of its armies stands ready to march"), and so does one with no city of the player's in reach ("none of its cities is near enough to yours to weigh a war on you"). Agreed after the eleventh play-through, whose "fallen to 0%" came after Macedon declared war on Carthage and read like a bug. A notice the player was never warned of ends in silence, one that ends in a war or a truce is told by them, one that comes back within the week is told by its own warning alone, and the line waits while the power is reckoned at the notice again by the day's end, as when guards stop counting that day. The page keeps the day a warned notice ended in its saved `told` as `threat-off:<power>:<day>` until the line is told. The line waits as the warning waits, because over 20 ten-year games of `npm run sim -- threat-runup` a line told the day the notice ended was followed by a new warning from the same power exactly 7 days later 5 times, and together they read as a broken promise; with the week's wait none comes within 7 days. Agreed after the tenth play-through, whose named day simply dropped off the At peace line. Over 20 ten-year games of `npm run sim -- threat-runup` it comes 3.6 times a game, 0 to 10, and of its 72 lines none is followed by a new warning from the same power within 7 days, 2 within 14 and 6 within 30, hence "for now"; told on the day the notice ended, it came 4.35 times a game, and 5, 14 and 17 of 87 were, with the warnings that stop the game unchanged at 6.6 a game. Agreed after the ninth play-through, in which Macedon warned in Year 1 and declared in Year 8 unwarned, because its calm never lasted the year that re-armed the old rule, and half the declarations came the day after the strength leapt past the warning. The warning and the At peace list say what lowers each threat, from the reckoning itself (`whatCounts` in `threat-news.ts`). A power that weighs a city counts every army of the player's on land within `WAR_FRONT_KM` (1,500 km) of it, its garrison, and its guards for their first `GUARD_DETER_DAYS`, and its line says so ("Your armies on land within 1,500 km count; more men, or guards at Capua for 90 days, lower it."), in no more lines of the city warning at 1280 × 720 than the line it replaced, because the tenth play-through read "your armies near Capua" as an order to march there, and a march from Roma changed nothing. A power that presses by rival pressure counts only the player's armies in the field, wherever they stand, and only while the player holds no more cities than it began with, so guards never lower it, and every city's Guards section names it and says so. Its line names the cheapest troop the player can afford (`raiseAdvice`) among those that finish training before the day the power may first declare, which it decides before that day's recruits muster, with its price and quickest training among the player's cities. With none affordable in time, it names the cheapest affordable troop to order now and the day it would muster and lower the threat, the first day war may come or so many days after, because men who muster on or after that day still count for as long as the power holds its notice below the strength it needs (`notice.test.ts`). Only with none affordable does it name the gold or manpower the player lacks for the cheapest, each named with its unit. Agreed after the eleventh play-through, whose At peace line said "none raised now is ready before war may come" from 10 days before the named day, though Skirmishers ordered then mustered on that day and ended the notice. A first warning leaves 23 days, so this shows mostly on the At peace list. A warning that comes later, as after a save is imported, names only the muster day ("Skirmishers ordered now muster on Year 1, day 111"), since it names the day war may come just before, and stays within its 12 lines at 1280 × 720. Once that day has passed, as in a reminder, there is no day to finish before, and it names what the player can raise, as before the tenth play-through: men raised then weigh for as long as the power holds its notice, which can be months, and "any day now" already says they may come too late. Agreed after the tenth play-through, whose warning advised Light cavalry that train in 20 days with 11 left, and read "you have 12" of manpower as days. Between warnings the wars panel's At peace list shows each great power at peace with the player and bound by no truce, with its share of the strength it needs in the same words (`standingThreats`). The top bar's Wars button counts the powers at `THREAT_WARN` from the same list every day. Each power's readiness is reckoned once a state (`readinessOf`), by the day's news inside the clock's frame, which costs a median 0.62 ms and a p95 of 1.05 ms a day for all four powers. The top bar and the panel then add a median 0.07 ms and a p95 of 0.13 ms a day, over 20 ten-year seeds in Node (`npm run sim -- threat-standing`). That run also showed that a news line when a power first reaches the full strength it needs would come a median of one day before its declaration, 45 of 50 within a day, so there is none; the warning instead ends by pointing to the Wars panel. A rule that re-armed on any dip below 0.6 warned up to four times in a game from one power, because its armies march off and come back within months. `npm run sim -- threat [-- --peaceful --guards never|monthly|every|warned|war]` plays the cautious Rome of `trade-judge --heed --improve` (from `scripts/cautious-rome.ts`; with `--peaceful` it declares no war) and prints the wars great powers declare on Rome, the cities it loses, its fate, the days its gold sits at its limit, and its longest stretch without a decision. `--guards` picks how it hires guards (`GuardPlay` in `scripts/cautious-rome.ts`): `every` hires at each 45-day check at every city a warning named, keeping a quarter of its gold limit, as the seventh play-through did; `warned` hires on a warning, and at a check where a power stands at `THREAT_WARN`, until no power stands at `THREAT_CLEAR` there, and fills the cities in a declaring power's reach; `war` hires three bands at the city nearest a declaring power and none at peace. Its `Summary:` line gives the row of the guards tables.
